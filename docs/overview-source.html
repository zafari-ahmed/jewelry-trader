One

What the system does

A piece is photographed and catalogued once. From then on, the same record serves the appraiser, the salesperson at the counter, the customer browsing online, and the bookkeeper at month end.

Jewelry Trader is built for dealers in antique, vintage, estate, designer and fine jewelry — where almost every item is unique, values are high, and provenance matters. It runs across several shops and several salespeople without the stock records drifting apart.

What makes it different from a general-purpose till

Built for unique stock

Most retail software assumes ten of everything. Here each piece is singular, so the system guarantees it cannot be sold twice — the shop and the website share one stock record, checked at the moment of payment.

Built for provenance

Condition, restoration history, hallmarks, gemstone detail and appraisal values travel with the piece, and price history is kept rather than overwritten.

Built for accountability

High-value trade attracts scrutiny. Every change is recorded against a person with a time and a reason, and the riskiest actions need a second person's approval.

Built to be adjusted by you

Tax rates, discount limits, commission structures, what each category must record, return windows and receipt wording are all settings — changed by your own team, without a developer.

Two

How a piece moves through the business

The path every item follows, from the moment it is acquired to the moment it leaves — and what happens if it comes back.

Two sets of eyes

Whoever catalogues a piece cannot publish it alone. A reviewer checks the record, then approves it — and listing it for sale is a second, separate decision.

Sold means sold

The moment payment succeeds, the piece is claimed. Two customers cannot buy the same ring, even if one is at the counter and the other is online at the same second.

Returns close the loop

A returned piece goes back on the shelf and reappears for sale automatically, with the money returned to however the customer paid.

Three

The item record

What the system holds about a piece — and how staff can see, at a glance, what is finished and what still needs work.

The traffic-light system

Every field on an item carries a colour. Staff do not have to remember what a bracelet needs versus what a ring needs — the record shows them.

ColourMeaningWhat it does
RedRequired, still emptyThe piece cannot be submitted until it is filled in.
YellowWorth verifyingFilled in, but flagged as needing a second look.
GreenCompleteTurns green by itself the moment a valid value is entered.
GrayNot applicableGreyed out for this kind of item — chain length on a ring, for instance.
BlueChanged by a personMarks where someone overrode a value, so the change is visible.
What each category requires is yours to decide. A ring can demand a size, a necklace a chain length, a watch a movement type — set from the settings screen, by your own staff, taking effect immediately.

What is captured

Identity and materials

Reference number, title, category, maker or brand, style period, metal type and purity, weight, measurements, hallmark.

Condition and history

Condition and restoration notes, internal remarks kept away from customers, and a provenance trail showing who did what, and when.

Gemstones

Stone type, shape, cut, colour, estimated carat weight and setting style — several stones per piece, with the principal stone marked.

Photography

Each shot tagged by what it shows — front, back, side, hallmark, gemstone, clasp, movement, signature, packaging, certificate — and one chosen as the image customers see first.

Valuation

Acquisition cost, retail price, insurance value, negotiation floor and promotional price. Changing a price keeps the old one on record rather than erasing it.

Descriptions

Separate wording for the customer, for search engines, for marketplaces and for social media — so each audience reads copy written for it.

Moving stock between shops

A transfer is requested, approved by the manager of the shop sending it, then confirmed on arrival. The piece is marked in transit in between, so it cannot be sold by either shop while it is in a courier's hands.

Four

At the counter

A full-screen till designed so an ordinary sale takes well under two minutes, with the awkward cases handled properly rather than worked around.

Handled properly

SituationHow it works
Services and repairsRing sizing, cleaning or a workshop job can be added as a line on the sale without being stock.
Paying two waysPart card, part cash, in any combination. The system will not let the parts add up to anything but the correct total, and works out the change.
DiscountsBy percentage or a fixed amount, per line. Past the limit you set, the sale needs a manager's authority.
ReturnsLook up the original sale, tick what is coming back. The refund includes the tax the customer paid and goes back to the card they used.
ExchangesSwap one piece for another in a single transaction — only the difference changes hands, in either direction.
Late returnsPast the return window you have set, the sale needs a manager's approval rather than being quietly refused.

Receipts appear on screen, print on standard 80 mm till rolls, and are emailed to the customer when an address is on file. The shop's details, the return window and the footer wording all come from your settings.

Five

The website

The same stock, the same prices, the same records — presented to customers, and kept in step with the shop floor automatically.

Customers keep their history

Someone can buy as a guest, then create an account later with the same email — and their earlier purchases are already there, not lost.

Card details never reach the shop

Card numbers are entered directly with the payment processor, in a form styled to match the shop. The business never holds them, which keeps card-security obligations at their lightest.

Sold in the shop, gone from the website. If a piece sells at the counter while someone has it in their online basket, it disappears from that basket rather than allowing a sale that cannot be honoured.
Six

Commission and pay

Four ways to structure commission, worked out automatically on every sale, and reported in a form payroll can use.

The four structures

StructureHow it is worked outExample on a $1,000 sale
On salesA flat percentage of the saleAt 5% — $50.00
On profitA percentage of what the piece made over what it costBought at $450, at 20% of margin — $110.00
TieredThe rate rises once a salesperson passes a threshold4% under $5,000, 6% above
SharedA pool split between everyone involved in the saleAt 5%, split 60/40 — $30.00 and $20.00

The rules it follows

  • Sales tax is never commissionable — it was never the shop's money.
  • Discounts reduce commission proportionally, so discounting costs the salesperson as well as the shop.
  • A shared pool always adds up exactly. Where a split does not divide evenly, the odd cents are distributed rather than lost — the parts always total the pool to the penny.
  • It never delays a customer. The calculation happens after the sale completes, in the background.
  • Anyone without their own agreement falls back to the standard arrangement you have configured.

Built around California wage law

Written agreementsEach salesperson's actual agreed terms are stored alongside the numbers, because the rate alone is not the agreement.
No clawbacksNothing automatically takes money back from commission already earned. A refund leaves the commission standing; any reduction must be raised, reasoned and approved by a manager.
Payment timingApproving a commission and paying it are recorded as separate, dated steps, so wage timing can be evidenced.

Reporting

Daily, weekly or monthly, filtered by salesperson, shop and status. Salespeople see their own figures; managers and bookkeepers see everyone's. The export for payroll can be shaped to whatever columns your payroll provider expects, without waiting on a developer.

Seven

Control and accountability

Who can do what, what needs a second person, and the record that sits behind all of it.

Six kinds of staff account

RoleWhat they can do
Owner / Super AdminEverything, including settings, staff accounts and approvals.
Shop ManagerStock, orders, approvals, discounts above the staff limit, and reports for their shop.
Sales StaffThe counter, creating and editing stock subject to approval, and their own commission.
AppraiserCataloguing and approving pieces for sale. No selling.
BookkeeperFinancial and commission reports, payment records, payroll export. No stock edits, no selling.
Customer ServiceOrders, customers and returns. No discounting, no price changes.

These are starting points, not fixed. You can create your own roles and choose exactly what each can do.

Protections around the risky actions

Two-factor sign-in

You choose which roles must use an authenticator app. Those staff are enrolled on first sign-in and challenged every time after. Nobody is exempt — not even the owner's account.

A second challenge for the riskiest actions

Approving an override or changing payment settings asks for a private symbol challenge only trusted staff know the answers to. Every attempt, successful or not, is recorded.

Overrides need a reason and approval

Selling below the floor price, discounting past the limit, releasing a held piece or accepting a late return all require a written reason and a second person's approval — including when the owner does it themselves.

Holds on individual pieces

A piece can be held from sale, from editing, from the website, or frozen entirely — for a valuation, a repair, a consignment dispute or a legal hold — with the reason shown to any member of staff who tries.

The record behind everything

Every change to a piece, an order, a payment, a customer or a setting is recorded automatically: who did it, when, from where, and what the value was before and after. Sign-ins, failed sign-ins and refused approvals are recorded too.

Financial records are kept for seven years to meet tax recordkeeping expectations, while general day-to-day activity is kept for a shorter, configurable period.

Nine

Prepared for what comes next

Four capabilities are already accounted for in the design, so adding them later is building on the foundation rather than disturbing it.
CapabilityWhat it will doAlready in place
Assisted cataloguing Suggest metal, period and stone detail from the intake photographs, draft customer descriptions, and propose a price band from comparable sales. The colours that mark a suggestion, a confirmation and a human correction already exist and already behave correctly.
Natural-language search Let customers search the way they speak — “art deco sapphire under five thousand”. Search sits behind a single point that can be swapped without changing the website.
Rentals Short-term hire of showcase pieces, with deposits, deductible options and return inspection. Agreements and claims are accounted for, and a piece can already be held back from rental specifically.
Salesperson shopfronts A personal storefront per salesperson, with their own branding and commission on what they refer. Shared commission already supports splitting a sale between people.
Quarterly stock audit Scheduled physical counts per shop, with variance reports and exceptions to investigate. Every stock movement is already recorded — which is exactly the data a reconciliation reads.

Why that matters commercially

Each of these was designed for from the start rather than bolted on afterwards. The parts of the system they will touch — how a suggestion is marked, how stock is held, how a sale is shared between people, how movements are recorded — already work the way those features will need them to.

In practice that means a shorter, lower-risk piece of work when you decide to add one, and no disruption to what is already running in the shop.
